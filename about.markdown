## Biography

I am an AI researcher at the **Centre for Research, Data Science and IT
Solutions** in Ho, and hold a PhD in Applied Statistics from the University of
Technology and Applied Sciences, Navrongo. My research develops neural-network
activation functions and lightweight deep-learning architectures for
classification and forecasting in resource-limited settings, alongside applied
work in econometrics, NLP for low-resource languages, and public-health
analytics.

I started in optimization, moved into statistical and mathematical modelling,
and now work on explainable AI and on climate and health. The applied problems
have varied a great deal — irrigation and crop allocation, epidemic
forecasting, maternal health, hepatitis B prevalence, monetary policy, machine
translation for Twi. What connects them is a concern with what can honestly be
inferred from the data actually to hand, and with whether the people expected
to act on a result have any reason to trust it.

None of that was planned. The COVID-19 work began because the data was
published daily and I wanted to see what it would support. The AI work began
with NLP Ghana, which led to the activation-function research that became my
PhD, which is where my interest in explainability started. I have found that
the useful problems tend to arrive through available data and good
collaborators rather than through a research strategy — and that saying so is
more honest than the tidier story I could tell instead.

## Selected experience

I have worked as a quantitative analyst with the **International AIDS Vaccine
Initiative**, as a research consultant to the **WHO**, the **Ghana Health
Service** and the **University of Health and Allied Sciences** on Ghana's
national COVID-19 behavioural survey, and as a data scientist at the Centre for
Research, Data Science and IT Solutions. Earlier, I coordinated monitoring and
evaluation across ten districts for USAID/AED/SHARP and UNHCR HIV/AIDS projects
in the Volta Region, and taught statistics and research methodology at the
University of Education, Winneba.
